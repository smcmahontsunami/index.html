Treat

Loosen and lift soil with chemistry and time before anything touches the vehicle.

Exit standardFull, even chemical coverage on every panel, wheel and tire, with soil visibly breaking down before the car reaches friction.

Key controls

ControlTargetHow we verify
Chemical sequenceHigh-pH presoak first, then low-pH stepAlkaline breaks down road film; acidic neutralizes it and lifts mineral soil.
ConcentrationProduct data-sheet dilution, confirmed by titrationWeekly titration of each product. Supply water pH and hardness shift results, so don't trust the injector setting alone.
Dwell~4–6 ft of travel from presoak to the next step (≈9 sec at a 100 CPH setting)Calculated per site from chain speed and arch spacing.
Chain speedFastest speed that still passes the test car, held steady all dayHourly setpoint log. Speeding up at peak and slowing off-peak makes quality uneven.
Nozzle coverageEvery nozzle flowing, even pattern, no dry stripesHourly visual check; timed flow test monthly. Replace nozzles above ~110% of rated flow.
Wheels & tiresWheel chemistry plus high-pressure blasting before frictionTest car shows no brake dust at exit.
TemperatureWarm supply water in cold weatherSeasonal set-up check. Warm the water, not the chemical drum.

Failure → what the customer sees

Low concentration or short dwell
Road-film haze on sides and rear
Plugged or misaligned nozzle
Same dirty stripe on every car
Over-concentration
Soapy film and streaks, worst on light colors
Empty drum or lost prime
Whole step missing; dull, dirty finish
Worn nozzles
Nothing visible; up to 30% wasted chemical

Checks

Daily
  • Drum levels; pumps primed
  • All presoak arches spraying evenly
Weekly
  • Titrate every product; adjust injectors
Monthly
  • Timed flow test on every nozzle

Measure

  • Chemical use per car, by product, vs. target
  • Chain speed actual vs. setpoint
  • Drum-level and no-flow alarms

Rule: When speed cuts dwell, apply more liquid and rely on friction. Don't raise concentration.

Clean

Remove the loosened soil with friction and rinse, without marking or damaging the vehicle.

Exit standardNo film, dirt bands, rocker-panel dirt or brake dust. No swirls, grease marks or damage. Rinsed to neutral pH.

Key controls

ControlTargetHow we verify
Brush speedWithin the equipment's RPM spec (foam runs faster than cloth)Weekly check. Outside spec risks vehicle or equipment damage.
Contact pressureCloth presses ~3–4 in into the vehicle; never enough to fold mirrorsMonthly penetration measurement. Less than 3 in means worn media.
Media conditionNo tears, matting or trapped gritDaily visual; weekly hands-on. Replace on wear and cars washed, not age.
LubricationFoaming brush detergent on the car before and during frictionHourly walk: brushes wet and foamed.
ContaminationZero oil or grease on the mediaDaily check. Grease on cloth transfers to paint.
Wrap retractRetracts as the vehicle passes the front quarter panelWatch wraps on a test car; mirrors are the top damage claim.
Car spacing3.5–7 ft between carsGives wraps room to reset between cars.
RinseFull high-pressure rinse coverage; ~pH 7 at exitQuarterly pH check on a test car.

Failure → what the customer sees

Grit in media / no lubrication
Swirl marks and fine scratches
Pressure too high / late retract
Folded or broken mirrors
Worn or mis-set rocker brush
Dirty lower panels
Torn media at one height
Repeating dirty band
Grease on media
Black marks on paint
Poor rinse
Chemical film and streaks

Checks

Daily
  • Test car before opening
  • Media visual: tears, grease
Weekly
  • Hands-on media check: fraying (cloth), cracks (foam)
  • Brush speed and pressure settings
Monthly
  • Deep clean; check for rust and loose parts; measure penetration

Measure

  • Brush motor load vs. each brush's baseline
  • Cars washed since last media change
  • Damage claims per 1,000 cars

Rule: Most damage claims trace to pressure and retract timing. Keep entrance and exit cameras running for every claim.

Protect

Seal the clean surface, rinse it spot-free and finish the tires. This is the shine the customer pays for.

Exit standardUniform gloss, water beading evenly, no spots or streaks. Tires evenly dressed with no sling on wheels or paint.

Key controls

ControlTargetHow we verify
SequenceProtectant / ceramic → drying agent → spot-free final rinseA protectant can't fix poor rinsing upstream.
Protectant doseFull rated doseUsage per car vs. target. An under-dosed ceramic still beads water but gives little shine or durability.
Final rinse waterDissolved solids under 10 ppm (under 20 acceptable; 5 or less fully spot-free)Weekly reading on the RO output, continuous if a sensor is installed.
Reclaim waterNever in the final rinsePlumbing check. Reclaim carries soap, dirt and minerals.
RO feedSoftened ahead of the membranesScale on membranes drives output out of spec.
Tire shine dose~0.8–0.9 oz per car, split front and rear tireUsage per car; test car shows even dressing.
Tire shine applicatorLevel, clean, shielded from dryer air, on rubber onlyA tilted manifold dumps product to one side.

Failure → what the customer sees

High dissolved solids in rinse
Water spots, worst on dark cars in sun
Under-dosed protectant
Looks fine at exit; shine fades fast
Tire shine overdose or tilted manifold
Sling on wheels and lower panels
Protectant line empty
Dull finish; water sheets, car exits wet
Reclaim crossover
Spots and film after drying

Checks

Daily
  • Test car: gloss, beading, tire shine
  • Protectant and tire shine levels
Weekly
  • RO output reading logged
  • Tire shine manifold level and clean
Quarterly
  • Full water panel: pH, hardness, dissolved solids, chlorine

Measure

  • Protectant and tire shine use per car vs. target
  • RO output ppm and recovery ratio
  • Spot and shine complaints per 1,000 cars

Rule: Beading alone doesn't prove protection. Confirm the dose per car.

Dry

Strip the water off before the car leaves. Drying is mostly decided upstream: water that beads blows off, water that sheets doesn't.

Exit standardRoof, hood, glass, mirrors and rear hatch free of standing water, with no re-wetting after the dryers.

Key controls

ControlTargetHow we verify
Water conditionWater beading as the car reaches the dryersWatch the roof at the dryer entrance. Sheeting water points to the drying agent, not the blowers.
PlacementFirst overhead blowers remove the bulk; the rest angled to sweep one wayOpposing airflows push water back onto dried areas.
Rear of SUVs and vansWater pushed down and off the back, not toward itTest with an SUV; the rear hatch is the most common wet spot.
Rinse spacingNo high-pressure rinse mist reaching the dryer zoneVisual on a running tunnel.
Nozzle distanceClose to the body (~6–8 in on contouring side nozzles)Air speed drops fast with distance.
Airflow pathClean intake and safety screens; no torn nozzles or air leaksWeekly screen cleaning; leaks fixed immediately.
Motor healthAmps within each blower's baseline; no noise or vibrationLow amps point to a blocked intake. Noise or vibration means bearings or impeller balance.
Chain speedLeaves enough time under the dryersTest-car dryness checked at peak speed.

Failure → what the customer sees

Drying agent missing or weak
Wet roof and hood; spots later
Rear blower angle wrong
Wet rear hatch on SUVs
Opposing flows / rinse mist
Car re-wets after drying
Blocked intake / torn nozzle
Gradually wetter cars, energy wasted
Speed too high
Wet cars at peak hours only

Checks

Hourly
  • All blowers running; exit cars dry
Weekly
  • Clean intake and safety screens
  • Inspect nozzles and air bags for tears
Monthly
  • Amp reading on every blower vs. baseline
  • Listen for bearing noise and vibration

Measure

  • Amps per blower vs. baseline
  • Blower runtime vs. cars washed
  • Wet-car complaints by hour of day

Rule: Fix water condition and blower placement before adding horsepower.
